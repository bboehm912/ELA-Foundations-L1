4. Phonological Foundations & Word Structure

MN 7.1.4.4/8.1.4.4
  • Phonemes: The smallest individual units of spoken sound in a language (e.g., the 3 distinct sounds in /k//a//t/).
  • Graphemes: The written letters or combinations of letters that represent spoken phonemes.
  • Phonological Awareness: Recognizing, isolating, and manipulating spoken sound structures before reading written words.
🔊 Sound-to-Symbol Mechanics

Phoneme Isolate & Blend

Key Insight: Strong readers use sound-pattern recognition to automatically decode complex academic vocabulary.

5. Morphological Analysis: Roots & Affixes

MN 7.1.4.4/8.1.4.4
  • Morpheme: The smallest unit of language that carries distinct meaning.
  • Root/Base Word: The core element containing the primary meaning (e.g., struct = to build).
  • Prefixes: Morphemes added to the beginning of a root (e.g., de-, re-, con-).
  • Suffixes: Morphemes added to the end of a root (e.g., -tion, -ive, -ture).
🌱 Morphology Tree

Prefix + Root + Suffix

Deconstruction Strategy: Strip affixes away from unfamiliar words to isolate the core meaning of the root.

6. Direct Evidence & Quote Citation Protocol

MN 7.3.1.1/8.3.1.1
  • Direct Citation: Pulling verbatim excerpts from text to back up your claims.
  • Citation Formatting: Always enclose exact words in quotation marks and cite the author or slide source.
  • No Naked Quotes: Never drop a quote into your response without introducing it or explaining its significance.
💬 Citation Sandwich

Lead-in → Quote → Analysis

7. Textual Analysis & Reader Response

MN 7.1.4.4/8.1.4.4
  • Surface Reading vs. Close Analysis: Reading for plot gets you started; reading for word choice and structure builds mastery.
  • Annotating Structure: Circle unknown roots, underline key arguments, and box structural transition markers.
  • Context Clues: Using surrounding paragraph logic to infer meanings of unfamiliar terms before checking a dictionary.
🔍 Close Reading Lens

Annotate → Infer → Synthesize

Active Reading: Good readers keep a pencil in hand to track morphological shifts across texts.

8. Guided Practice: Deconstructing Words

MN 7.1.4.4/8.1.4.4
  • Example Word: Deconstruction
  • Prefix: de- (down, away from)
  • Root: struct (to build)
  • Suffix: -tion (state or process of)
  • Synthesized Meaning: The process of breaking down a built structure into its component parts.
🧩 Word Breakdown

[de] + [struct] + [tion]

9. Evaluating Diction & Tone

MN 7.1.4.4/8.1.4.4 & 7.1.5.5/8.1.5.5
  • Diction: The deliberate choice of words made by an author or speaker.
  • Denotation: The explicit, dictionary definition of a word.
  • Connotation: The emotional or cultural associations attached to a word (e.g., frugal vs. cheap).
🎨 Connotation Spectrum

Negative → Neutral → Positive

Diction Spotlight: Notice how choosing academic morphemes changes a writer's authority and tone.

10. Academic Claim Construction Baseline

MN 7.3.1.1/8.3.1.1
  • Claim Definition: A clear, arguable statement that answers a specific academic prompt.
  • Non-Examples: "I think words are important" or "Prefixes are cool."
  • Academic Example: "Understanding Greek and Latin root morphemes enhances decoding efficiency when encountering novel academic vocabulary."
⚡ Claim Formula

Specific Topic + Precise Stance

Standard Rule: Claims must always be supported by explicit textual evidence and clear reasoning.
